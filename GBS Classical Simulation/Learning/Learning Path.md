# GBS Learning Path — From Foundations to a Proposal

Created October 8, 2026. These are teaching notes, not new research results.

The goal is to explain the project in your own words, recognize what its evidence supports, and independently draft or revise a defensible proposal. This follows the format of the QSP learning sequence: one concept at a time, small examples, exercises, answer checks, and a mastery checkpoint.

## Which project are we learning?

The active direction is an independent reproduction and benchmark of a published phase-space sampler on Jiuzhang 4.0. The earlier selective-correlation study provides preliminary experience and an important negative performance result. It is a separate method, not the implementation of the active proposal. See [[Sources and Project Map]] for the exact project files and papers.

## How to use the lessons

Start with Lesson 00. Use 30–50 minutes for a first session, plus time to attempt the exercises; this is a suggested pace, not a deadline. Each lesson ends with a small proposal-writing task. Keep those paragraphs in your own working proposal and revise them as your understanding improves.

Suggested tutor prompt: “Teach me Lesson 03 from the GBS Classical Simulation/Learning folder in my Quantum Research vault. Explain simply, work through an example, and ask one question at a time. Let me try before showing answers.”

Reading is preparation. A checkpoint passes when you can explain the idea and solve an unfamiliar example without copying the notes. Skip a foundation only after doing its checkpoint. Mathematical extensions are optional for a first proposal draft; understanding the claim boundaries is essential.

## Sequence

| Lesson | Central question | Proposal skill |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [[00 - The Project and Its Claims]] | What are we actually testing? | Separate motivation, hypothesis, and completed evidence. |
| [[01 - Probability and Click Records]] | What does it mean to reproduce random outputs? | Define the output task and target distribution. |
| [[02 - The Optical System]] | How does light become a click string? | Describe squeezing, interference, loss, and detection. |
| [[03 - Gaussian States and Covariance]] | How can a compact state describe a huge distribution? | Specify the model and its conventions. |
| [[04 - Correlations and Hidden Differences]] | What information do local statistics miss? | Explain the limits of validation. |
| [[05 - Exact and Approximate Sampling]] | Which calculations become expensive? | Describe methods and relevant competitors. |
| [[06 - Phase Space and the Proposed Sampler]] | How does the active classical method work? | Explain the method and its approximation. |
| [[07 - The Preliminary Study and Its Audit]] | What did our earlier experiment establish? | Write an honest preliminary-evidence paragraph. |
| [[08 - Validation and Statistical Uncertainty]] | When is a mismatch meaningful? | Choose tests, uncertainty estimates, and decision rules. |
| [[09 - Reproduction and Fair Comparisons]] | How do we make the test credible? | Define artifacts, gates, baselines, and held-out evaluation. |
| [[10 - Scaling and Resource Budgets]] | What will the project cost? | Justify compute, memory, storage, and milestones. |
| [[11 - Drafting and Defending the Proposal]] | Can we turn understanding into a research plan? | Draft and review a complete proposal. |

Lessons 00–03 build the physical and probability language. Lessons 04–07 connect it to the algorithms and actual project history. Lessons 08–11 prepare the evaluation and writing.

## Final readiness test

Explain the target, proposed method, approximation, validation limits, prior results, reproduction gate, cost comparison, and possible failure outcomes in five minutes. Then produce a proposal whose key factual claims each have a source and whose future results remain phrased as questions or hypotheses. Lesson 11 supplies the worksheet and review rubric.
